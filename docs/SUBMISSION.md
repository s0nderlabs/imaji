## Name

imaji

## One line

One release in, a whole launch kit out. A job your Mind can take.

## Track

Track 2, content repurposing across platforms.

## Description

A developer who ships is a creator whose content is code. Every release they tag is a launch nobody writes up.

imaji is not an agent and it is not a wrapper. It is a job description any Mind can take, plus the hands to do it. The developer hands their own Mind (Minds by Animoca Brands) the job once, in prose, in a chat. From then on every GitHub release they tag wakes that Mind through a single workflow file, and nobody prompts it again. The Mind reads the release, remembers the brand and every release before this one, decides what this release earns (a typo fix earns nothing, and it says so in the developer's voice), writes every word (the tweet, the three-part thread, the LinkedIn post, the card headline, the film lines, the storyboard for the launch video), and calls imaji to render.

imaji renders and hosts. It turns the Mind's words into a 1200 x 630 card, a ten-second film, a vertical cut for Shorts and Reels, and, when the release is a launch, a 30 to 45 second launch video built from the repository's own screenshots and a live capture of its homepage, all from HTML so the output is deterministic and on brand. Then it puts the kit on a private page. It has no memory, no taste and no judgment of its own.

Configuration is a conversation: the developer corrects the Mind in one sentence and the correction holds. An optional three-key `imaji.json` in the repository holds standing preferences, and chat still wins over it. A coding agent can do the whole setup from one prompt.

## Why a Mind, and not an API call

- It is triggered by your work, not by you. The Action wakes it; you never prompt it.
- It remembers. Every release, every correction, every colour you changed. Kit two says "building on v0.1.0" without being told.
- It refuses. Trivial releases are skipped with a reason, in your voice.

## Minds Integration Depth

The Mind is the employee. It holds the brief, the memory and every decision; imaji holds a renderer and a page. The Mind takes the job from plain prose rather than a config file, is onboarded from the repository itself and proposes the brand, reads the release with `HTTP_Execute`, chooses the outputs, writes the copy, directs the launch video beat by beat, and POSTs the kit to imaji's render endpoint. Each developer runs their own Mind: Episodes and Tenets are per Mind, so one person's brand can never reach another's kit. Pull the Mind out and what remains is a template filler with no history and no taste, which anyone can verify by reading the repository.

## Persistence

Memory and autonomy, in that order. `imaji.labs`, the Mind that announces imaji, took the job on 28 August 2026, was onboarded from this repository, kept a brand correction across conversations, and then handled v0.1.0, v0.2.0 and v0.3.0 through the workflow without being prompted, each kit referencing the one before it. Asked in a fresh conversation what it remembered, it listed all three. It also skipped a release on its own, saying "a one line README typo is no user facing change, so it earns no kit". The only thing anyone typed was the release notes. Releases are the trigger, not the only door: asked in chat for a launch video or a fresh card, the Mind calls imaji the same way and the result lands on the same kit page.

## Creator-Economy Problem Fit

Solo builders who only ship are creators with no time to be creators. They already write the release notes; what they do not do is the tweet, the thread, the post, the card and the video, every time, in one voice. imaji moves that work to the Mind they already have, at the moment they were already going to tag a release, and posts nothing on their behalf.

## Innovation and Creativity

The inversion is the idea: not a pipeline with a model in the middle, but a Mind with a pipeline for hands. Two consequences fall out of it. Visuals are HTML rendered frame by frame rather than generated by a diffusion model, so a brand comes out exact and repeatable. And the launch video is storyboarded by the Mind, beat by beat, from the repository's own pictures and a live screenshot of the product.

## Execution and Completeness

It runs, and it announced itself. Three releases, six outputs, a public site, a docs site, a job description, a workflow template, an agent setup path, an adversarial review whose findings were fixed before 0.3.0, tests and typechecks, and an offline check that parses the workflow. Card in about a second, film in about twelve, launch video in about a minute; a full kit is two to four minutes of Mind time.

## Viability and Scalability

Every user brings their own Mind and their own credit, so nothing about the design assumes a shared brain or a shared bill. The render side is a small stateless service with flat files behind it. Setup is one pasted job description, one workflow file and three secrets. A Bazaar listing comes later, so that taking the job is a Skill a Mind can pick up rather than a paragraph a person has to paste.
